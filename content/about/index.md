I'm a Computer Science graduate student at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, advised by Prof. [Kassem Fawaz](https://kassemfawaz.com/). I work on usable security and privacy, and I build systems that address security and privacy problems in interpersonal relationships.

Most security research assumes the adversary is a stranger. But a lot of the harm people actually experience comes from someone close to them — a partner or a family member, someone who already knows their passwords and shares their home. My work focuses on that setting: what those threats look like in practice, why conventional defenses miss them, and what we can build instead.

## What I work on

**Technology-facilitated intimate partner abuse.** With Rahul Chatterjee and colleagues, I studied how everyday automation apps — iOS Shortcuts, Tasker, IFTTT, Samsung Modes & Routines — can be repurposed for surveillance and harassment, and built a detector that surfaced 1,014 abusable recipes among 12,962 publicly shared ones. ([USENIX Security 2025](/abusability-ipv/))

**Deceptive design at scale.** I work on automatically detecting deceptive patterns on the web, delivered as a browser extension for users, a Lighthouse audit for developers, and a measurement tool for researchers and regulators. ([CCS 2025](https://wiscprivacy.com/deceptive-patterns/) — Distinguished Paper Award)

**Privacy and AI in the home.** I study how well large language models track people's privacy expectations: for in-home robots making decisions on someone's behalf, and for families using conversational AI together.
