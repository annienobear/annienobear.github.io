I'm a Computer Science Ph.D. student at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, advised by Prof. [Kassem Fawaz](https://kassemfawaz.com/) in the [Wisconsin Privacy and Security Group](https://wiscprivacy.com/).

My research lies at the intersection of technical HCI, privacy, and AI-driven robotics. I build trustworthy systems that model the social dynamics of multi-user environments, using qualitative methods and social science theory alongside system building.

## What I work on

My dissertation asks whether GenAI is ready to understand household privacy norms and make privacy decisions on people's behalf — and if not, what it would take to get there. Privacy at home isn't an individual setting: accounts, devices, and now embodied agents get shared by people whose preferences differ.

- **Characterizing the threat** — how GenAI is used, mediated, and misused by people sharing accounts and devices ([Family Relations 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1111/fare.13171), [arXiv:2504.09004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09004)).
- **Measuring whether GenAI can reason about privacy** — a Contextual Integrity benchmark where 450 people set the human baseline ([arXiv:2507.16124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16124)). Models aren't ready to decide alone, but improve sharply with a human in the loop.
- **Building defenses** — privacy *auditors* that detect harms under human oversight, like the detector that found 1,014 abusable automation recipes among 12,962 public ones ([USENIX Security 2025](/abusability-ipv/)); an on-device privacy *controller* is the proposed direction.
